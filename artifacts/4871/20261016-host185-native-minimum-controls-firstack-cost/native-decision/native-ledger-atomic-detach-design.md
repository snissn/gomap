# Canonical reservation commit/detach decision

Disposition: **ACCEPT as a conditional source design direction, not implementation acceptance or a runtime grant.** The smallest safe split keeps the original canonical reservations conservatively excluding their pages until the SAME publication receipt completes charged ledger retirement. Durable commit does not require deleting every RAM reservation first. Moving `PublishBatch` unchanged to Finish does not provide bounded progress: burned-range storage and its producers must also change.

## What the current transition actually does

`freelist/generation_v1_reservation.go:623` validates a batch through a newly allocated seen map, deletes every reservation ID from `owners`, and for each candidate filters all `burnedTails` against all of its `abandonedCoverage` intervals. Thus its work is at least proportional to IDs plus burned-ranges times coverage. The burned slice is compacted in place. This is canonical allocator behavior, used by native scoped publication and ordinary publication; it is not private diagnostic bookkeeping.

`allocator_publication_work_v6.go:47–133` holds allocator then ledger locks, prices those actual loops before effects, and retains those locks through the authority scope. Its large admission is honest for the present operation. `allocator_cow.go:523–600` then calls the ledger transition, marks prepared candidates published, and detaches the activated prefix. Native head reslicing avoids copying the suffix, but still clears the selected prefix; general cost is O(selected prefix), not universal O(1). B1 makes that particular count fixed.

`db/durable_primary_runtime_v5.go:714–977` makes allocator publication the last fallible canonical transition before installing `db.durableRoot`, slot/resource ownership and meta-page identity, detaching debt, and transferring original retired descriptors/seals to the original visible member. `primary_member_retirement_work_v6.go` later drives that member's genuine retirement, including the original seal release continuation. This is the appropriate existing Finish seam. `native_mvcc_published_terminal_v6.go` runs after original receipt Finish; adding ledger cleanup only there would incorrectly allow the receipt to complete first.

## Recommended coherent representation and transition

1. **Prepare the exact handoff before effects.** Extend the original reservation/containing seal's existing cleanup state, admission and typed Finish child. Preserve the exact reservation pointer, candidate identity, immutable ID vector and abandoned-coverage operands. Allocate/admit all added cursor/link storage before physical writes/adoption. No history-sized plan is constructed inside the atomic commit and no new global owner is introduced.

2. **Commit logically under the existing locks.** Validate the exact original activated head and exact reservation objects, then change each selected original reservation to a distinct committed-pending-Finish disposition. Attach/transfer its cleanup obligation to the SAME existing seal/member receipt before any external return. Mark the original prepared candidate published, detach only that exact activated prefix, and install the original durable cut/slots/resources before releasing the authority scope/turn. There must be no fallible allocation, budget refusal, or independently observable gap between that handoff and the durable-root installation. Previously admitted genuine I/O failure keeps the original unfenced/ambiguous policy and custody; it does not manufacture successful commitment.

3. **Keep exclusions conservative.** Leave `owners[id]` and the reservation's tail exclusion present until Finish removes them. Existing `reservedLocked`, `reservedByOtherLocked`, range-conflict, append and metadata-reuse consumers can continue treating them as reserved. Do not change all consumers to ignore a committed flag. Committed candidates cannot be superseded, rolled back, retried as unpublished, or reused by candidate ID while cleanup remains. Any generic transition accepting them needs an explicit disposition. A current-generation free bit is not a substitute for reservation or old-reader/slot reuse authority.

4. **Make burned ranges stable at their original producer.** Replace the anonymous compacted burned interval slice with stable, canonically linked burn descriptors. An original failed reservation can supply an embedded descriptor containing its already captured tail range; transfer that descriptor to the SAME ledger when Fail/previsible rollback burns a written non-reused tail. This avoids allocating a replacement descriptor on a physical-error path. The descriptor's whole retained containing allocation and any still-live vectors require truthful original custody/charge; embedding is not a refund exemption. Assign a checked monotonic identity before the first write can require a burn; candidate-ID reuse or pointer address alone is insufficient identity. Ordinary and native producers/removers use this one representation. Preserve existing semantics that any burned tail excludes allocation until a durable generation's immutable abandoned coverage proves it covered.

5. **Finish actual work with persistent progress.** One admitted step deletes one ID only while the canonical candidate entry still names the SAME reservation and `owners[id]` still names its original candidate. Persist its index in the original receipt child. For burned coverage, use an original receipt-embedded moving list marker and fixed stop marker installed under the ledger lock at logical commit; append after the stop is conservatively left for a later publication. Markers are intrusive traversal links in the existing ledger, not another ownership registry. Ordinary removers must preserve them. Each step inspects the moving marker's next stable burn identity, compares one original coverage operand, and persists scalar comparison progress; if another remover has removed that node, restart only that node's comparison for the new identity. No borrowed burn pointer survives the unlock. After a node is retained or removed, move the marker past it in O(1). On reaching its own stop, remove both original markers. Concurrent receipt markers are admitted traversal visits, not reasons to wait for another Finish. This is one concrete bounded-storage implementation option requiring focused review; the necessary property is stable producer-maintained mutation-safe progress, not this particular marker spelling.

6. **Complete only after real retirement.** Once IDs and the selected burned snapshot are processed, unlink the exact committed candidate and clear its original aliases before refunding their actual backing. The original seal Finish child must run before `primary_seal_release_work_v6.go:222–end` clears `prefix`, releases dependent manifest metadata, or reports the containing original receipt complete. Failure retains the same linked receipt/error graph and charged cleanup state. No synthetic receipt, early ACK, or private cleanup owner is acceptable. Ordinary synchronous publication may drive the SAME canonical child to completion with its existing policy; native work must yield through the real child.

The markers add constant control storage per original outstanding publication, not per burned range or history. They still require original constructor admission, alias-before-refund and error/Close lifecycle proof. A simpler stable list with a saved pointer but no mutation/lifetime protocol does not suffice.

## Durable meaning and concurrency

The committed generation and root record, not the RAM ledger cleanup, define recovery authority. `generation_v1_emit_work.go:89–170` marks tail-write intent before writing; `generation_v1_txn.go` materializes immutable reservation extents. `generation_v1_format.go:316–418` reloads and validates those extents, generation identity/digest and exact metadata ownership. Reopen constructs a fresh RAM ledger rather than recovering its `owners` map. Therefore delaying conservative RAM exclusion removal does not require a disk-format change. It must not change what extents the published generation proves, capsule/root durability ordering, recovery slot closure, or retained-reader reuse capability.

A later ordinary writer may append/claim other pages while old exclusions remain. Delayed cleanup can reduce reuse or force append, and at resource limits can reduce availability; unrestricted continuing-writer progress is **not** proved by this split. A writer cannot take an excluded ID early. A later failed tail appended after a receipt's stop remains excluded. Old reader/slot physical retirement still follows its existing independent capability/last-edge path. Partially finished original receipts survive error/Close through their existing custody; no callback can see their storage refunded first.

## Counterexamples that rule out smaller flag-only changes

* Marking `published` and later calling unchanged `PublishBatch` merely moves its indivisible length-dependent scan; it cannot make a Q32 Finish step.
* Saving `burnedTails[i]` or an index across yields is invalid: an ordinary publish can compact earlier entries or append/reallocate, so the saved index skips a remaining range or identifies another range. Holding the lock across public yields/I/O is not a solution.
* If a committed flag makes an ID reusable before old cleanup, an unconditional later `delete(owners,id)` can remove a successor's real claim. Either keep conservative exclusions as recommended, or change canonical owner values/consumers to retain exact reservation identity and perform identity-checked deletion; that is additional producer work.
* Clearing the original `prefix`, reservation IDs/coverage, or containing admission before its ledger child completes loses the actual work operands or leaves an uncharged alias. A boolean `ledgerDone` without actual traversal is not completion.
