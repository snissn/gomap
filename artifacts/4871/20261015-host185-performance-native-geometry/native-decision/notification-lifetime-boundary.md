## Finding and consequence

Pinned Go1.26.3 DWARF resolves `runtime.hchan`112, `runtime.timeTimer`112, embedded `runtime.timer`96, `time.Time`24 and public `time.Timer`16. This resolves representation-size opacity for that compiler/architecture. It does not identify the final consumer of an individual channel/timer. Current coordinator code has no retained owner or release receipt for these scheduling outputs and no registered borrows by readers. Therefore a complete per-operation retained-allocation/alias-lifetime plan cannot be implemented by adding Reserve calls alone.

`notifyLocked` (`coordinator.go:1286`) closes the old `c.changed` and allocates a new unbuffered channel. The new channel is stored on the SAME existing coordinator. Native Bind (`native_publication_v6.go:83`), BeginTurn (`:127`), failure disposition (`:169`) and terminal Complete (`:251`) explicitly call it; EnqueueBuilt (`admission.go:140`) and successful ReleaseTurn through `completePublicationLockedV6` (`primary_terminal_completion_v6.go:77`) do too. These native-specific notifications add actual control allocations whose lifetime cannot be described as an already-owned DATA allocator output. The original channel operations and object traffic must remain charged, under the root-approved diagnostic boundary.

Two source-backed counterexamples prevent early refund:

1. An admission/drain/Stop reader copies `changed := c.changed` under c.mu, unlocks, and can be descheduled before or within select. Native notify closes/replaces that channel; native publication and Finish complete before the reader resumes. Its local alias is still live. Neither replacing the channel nor completing the original publication proves the old allocation is no longer referenced.
2. Terminal `CompleteNativePublicationV6` itself allocates a new channel and leaves it in `c.changed` after returning. Even with NO external observers in the controlled fixture, this newest allocation survives actual Finish until a future notification or coordinator destruction. Refunding it with the producer/frame at Finish is false accounting. Closing it early changes the coordinator wake contract and would be a notification behavior change.

The second counterexample means the existing no-concurrent-verifier/no-new-writer final-turn schedule alone cannot close this lifetime. The first becomes relevant as soon as the required newer real foreground writer/Close/Stop or other ordinary consumer may run. Holding c.mu cannot join a descheduled borrower; waiting while holding c.mu would deadlock a borrower that must reacquire the same lock. No existing scalar geometry field counts these aliases.

## Timer is the same class of boundary

`installTimerLocked` invokes original Clock.Now/publishDelay and `realClock.NewTimer`; `requestPublishLocked` Stop()s and nils the current timer; `handleTimerFiredLocked` also nils it. The scheduler may still retain a copied receive channel outside c.mu when either happens. Go's Stop semantics prohibit stale deliveries; they do not erase the scheduler's local channel pointer. The timer-channel/runtime-timer relation is also source-backed: runtime.hchan has the timer pointer used by syncTimer/NewTimer. Therefore clearing c.timer or Stop returning is not proof of last alias.

Pinned source supports pricing actual layouts without a runtime mirror: `time/sleep.go:NewTimer` allocates a cap1 chan time.Time then newTimer; `runtime/chan.go:makechan` selects backing by pointer-bearing element; `runtime/time.go:newTimer` allocates timeTimer. The public16-byte time.Timer is the prefix of the112-byte runtime allocation, not an additional independent timer object. time.Time contains a Location pointer, so channel element backing must follow the actual pointer-bearing branch; do not blindly combine112+24 into one allocation charge. `realTimer` returned behind Timer interface also needs source/compiler escape analysis before claiming its actual allocation count. No new compile/runtime was launched merely to infer it. The ordinary runtime scheduler's global heap remains service under the accepted boundary; this does not erase explicitly native-created channel/timer operands.

## Strongest maintained ownership primitive and mismatch

The existing `retainedalloc.Owner` Add/AddPending and RemovalWork/EnrollmentReleaseWork mechanisms can admit real backing before creation and refund after the caller clears its last edge. They cannot discover arbitrary Go local channel aliases or the current coordinator edge. Existing NativePublicationV6 is the SAME physical receipt and can retain its original frame until Finish, but its terminal phase is not last use of c.changed. Existing primaryTerminalCompletionV6 holds original transaction accounting AFTER callbacks return; it likewise has no channel observer edge or retirement proof. StableResourceToken/Set describe physical file/namespace authority and are not a notification lifetime mechanism; enrolling scheduling channels there would introduce an unrelated authority graph.

The smallest coherent exact-accounting construction would require a NEW SHARED NOTIFICATION LIFETIME SEAM: per-native-born notification/timer operand metadata, a current-coordinator ownership edge, explicit borrow/drop at the four channel capture sites and scheduler timer capture, replacement/Stop drops, and terminal scrubbing/refund only after every current/borrowed edge is gone. It must carry original retained owner/charge after the producing publication finishes, without retaining its resource/root/callback frame. That means new independently surviving control metadata, with its own admitted size and finite cleanup. Its integration includes ordinary notify/borrow paths as well as native notifications. It cannot be hidden inside a scalar Reserve, a callback wrapper, or a fixture-only promise. A counter-only scheme without a per-operand identity cannot distinguish older borrowed channels from the newest current one across multiple replacements.

This is smaller than a general registry and need not index anything globally, but it is still the NEW ownership protocol/framework boundary the coordinator explicitly required this lane to stop and report before construction. Adding it silently would also change ordinary shared-path layout/cost; a nil-metadata branch and corresponding containing sizeof charges would need explicit ordinary reconciliation and qualification. A count-until-coordinator-Close list would accumulate history and is rejected. A forced observer join before Finish or channel close would change continuing-write/ACK/scheduler semantics and is rejected. Pools of independently retained handles are rejected.
