## Errors and the owner-progress choice

**Unchanged retry policy alternative.** The successful prefix/waiter change alone is insufficient. Native pre-fence failure must preserve original pending/candidate/receipt; release activeAttempt/publishing and record a retry result, without private cancellation or success. The original owner must later explicitly resume the SAME candidate, never re-Enqueue or rerun visibility/Request consumption. A new attempt captures current waiters/drains BEFORE physical effects and reserves actual capture work. To move terminal failure delivery out of F, extend the original attempt with a separately admitted immutable settlement cell. During pre-effect capture, bind each captured original waiter/drain to that exact cell; late arrivals remain uncaptured. Seal its result in O(1) and notify. Callers consume that exact error even if newer attempts/frontiers occur before they wake. Do not overwrite a latched old failure, use latest-error generation as a substitute, or introduce a failure-history/keeper registry. Capture may traverse W+D before effects and refuse without taking a turn; it is not constant work or Q32 progress proof. Original ordinary capture can remain eager, but mixed consumers must distinguish the exact latched result.

A settlement cell must not borrow a refundable cursor/transaction/candidate. Charge any new native backing before allocation/attachment, retain it until last captured caller releases, and perform its actual accounting continuation after alias detachment. Alternatively an embedded cell keeps its containing receipt charged and prevents reusing it until all captured users release; that restricts retry progress. All such costs belong in F/retained accounting. A no-new-cell design can keep the original pre-effect captured arrays and move their terminal delivery into an explicitly separate original service task that retains the SAME attempt until delivery completes; that is additional retained original service state and requires its own lifetime proof, not an uncharged queue.
